Written for: you, plus anyone (or any later session) working in this repo, since docs/DESIGN.md lives there.

I saved the design to docs/DESIGN.md and finished Phases 0 and 1. All 17 physics tests and 4 app tests pass, and the analyzer is clean. I couldn't run it on a phone or as a Mac app because Xcode isn't installed here, so I ran it in headless Chrome at phone size. A drag-and-fling on the clapper and sail produced 11 hits across 3 of the 5 tubes, with 0 resets and no console errors.

What's there

Physics package in packages/chime_sim/, pure Dart with no Flutter or Flame imports.
3D particle physics, 4 substeps per 120 Hz step.
Tubes whose lengths come from their pitch: C5 works out to 45.5 cm for an 18 mm aluminium tube.
Clapper–tube collisions; holding the clapper against a tube doesn't keep re-triggering hits.
Hits are reported through a fixed-size event buffer that never allocates.
A speed limit and automatic reset if the numbers ever go invalid.
Game layer in lib/game/: the Flame loop, a 2.5D renderer that draws back to front and flashes a tube when it's struck, and drag/fling input. lib/main.dart locks portrait.
Debug overlay in lib/ui/overlays/debug_panel.dart: fps, physics steps per frame, and each hit's tube, impulse, speed and strike point. It's hidden in release builds.
What the tests cover

The rest pose is stable.
A fling toward each tube hits that tube first.
A clapper held against a tube goes silent once it settles.
Energy only decreases after a fling.
10 simulated minutes of random grabs, tilts and shaking stay finite, under the speed limit, and with every string intact.
Bugs the testing caught

The clapper could escape the ring. The 45 mm clapper fit through the 46.7 mm opening between two tubes, with 0.8 mm to spare. I changed the ring radius to 5.2 cm and the clapper to 50 mm (7 mm too big to pass), added a test, and updated the design doc.
The hook was drawn 9 px lower than intended because of the camera tilt. Fixed and tested.
Things to know

Run it on a real phone. Install Xcode for iOS, or connect an Android device and run ~/fvm/versions/stable/bin/flutter run (the SDK isn't on your PATH). Touch feel and frame rate on a phone are what count.
I left out Riverpod for now. The design lists it, but there are no controllers for it to manage until Phases 4–5.
Running the web check needed a Node install (playwright-core) and a small driver script in my scratch folder. Running /run-skill-generator would save that as a reusable project skill.
Next is Phase 2: the wind generator, a manual wind slider in the debug panel, a placeholder "ding" through flutter_soloud, and a headless script to tune hit rates against wind speed.
